Florida Home Insurance Relief: What to Check on Your Own Policy

Statewide savings do not tell you the premium or protection for your house. Start with the original sources, then compare coverage, deductibles, wind documentation, and flood timing.

Quick answer, as of September 2026

The August 2026 Moore-Lynch report combines homeowners and auto premiums in its nearly $3 billion Florida decline; it does not establish your renewal savings. Citizens’ April 30 notice lists July average decreases of 8.8% for homeowners multiperil and 5.1% for wind-only. Compare your actual premium, coverage, deductibles, and effective dates with your insurance agent.

Key takeaways

  • Separate statewide premium totals from your own renewal offer.
  • Compare coverage and hurricane deductibles alongside the annual premium.
  • Ask which wind-mitigation documents and credits the insurer actually used.
  • Confirm flood coverage, binding conditions, and effective dates before closing or cancellation.

Florida home insurance relief in 2026 deserves a closer look than a headline about billions saved. A statewide premium total, an insurer's average rate change, and the renewal bill for your house measure different things. The useful question for a Pensacola, Gulf Breeze, or Navarre homeowner is whether a specific policy offers suitable coverage at a cost the household can carry.

This guide checks the original industry report and Citizens' implementation notice, then turns them into a renewal-comparison worksheet. Use our Florida home insurance resource for the broader coverage questions. Here, the goal is to decide what to compare, what documents to collect, and what still needs an agent's answer before you change policies or close on a house.

What does the Florida insurance relief report actually say?

The August 2026 Moore-Lynch report, commissioned by the American Property Casualty Insurance Association, reports that Florida homeowners direct written premiums fell $1.29 billion, or 7%, in 2025. It separately reports a $1.70 billion, or 5%, decline for private passenger auto. The nearly $3 billion combined figure is therefore not a homeowners-only saving.

The original report, pages 12-13, also lists a 0.9% homeowners rate increase for 2025. A smaller increase is still an increase. The report was commissioned by an insurance trade association and uses regulatory and filing data accessed through S&P Global. Its aggregate results do not establish the change in a particular homeowner's bill.

When you read a broad “insurance is falling” claim, identify the measure before applying it to your budget. Total premiums can reflect the policies written and their coverage as well as pricing. Your renewal also needs to be compared with your own expiring policy, not with an unrelated statewide total.

  • Aggregate premiums: dollars reported across the measured market.
  • Average rate change: a pricing measure for the stated product and period.
  • Your renewal: the actual proposed premium, coverage, deductibles, and conditions.

What did Citizens change for July 2026?

Citizens' April 30, 2026 implementation bulletin lists average decreases of 8.8% for homeowners multiperil and 5.1% for homeowners wind-only business, effective July 1 for new and renewal policies. Use the dated Citizens bulletin to identify the product and effective date before comparing your own offer.

The later bulletin's wind-only figure differs from the 5.5% in Citizens' earlier March announcement. This guide uses the later implementation notice. Neither statewide average tells you the price of a particular Escambia or Santa Rosa County home, and a private insurer's policy is a different quote altogether.

Ask the agent to reconcile the old and new declarations pages. If the premium rises, identify what changed in coverage, deductibles, discounts, fees, or the property's rating information. If it falls, do the same review before assuming the new policy provides identical protection.

How do you compare two home insurance quotes fairly?

Put the coverage and deductible beside the premium. A cheaper annual bill may be attractive, but the decision is incomplete until you understand the loss you would need to fund yourself. This original worksheet uses hypothetical offers for the same house; these are not available quotes or a forecast of local pricing.

The example applies each percentage to the stated dwelling coverage. Florida's hurricane-deductible statute addresses deductibles based on policy dwelling limits. Confirm the basis, calendar-year rules, and other terms in your actual policy with the agent. Do not calculate a percentage deductible from the premium or the home's purchase price.

The table does not say either policy is better. It exposes the tradeoff: an annual premium saving and a larger amount to absorb if the applicable loss occurs. That is not a forecast of claims or a way to calculate expected annual loss. The household's available reserves and the rest of the coverage matter.

Then compare the items the table cannot capture: covered causes of loss, roof settlement provisions, water exclusions, additional living expenses, liability limits, endorsements, and any separate flood coverage. Ask the agent to explain differences in writing. A matching dwelling limit alone does not make two contracts equivalent.

Which wind-mitigation documents should you collect?

Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation says the updated OIR-B1-1802 wind-mitigation form took effect April 1, 2026. Its wind-mitigation resources explain the form, inspection requirements, and the conditions for relying on an existing inspection for up to five years. Material changes or inaccuracies can affect that reliance.

Gather the report, roof records, permits where available, and documentation for shutters or other opening protection. Ask the agent which features were recognized in the quote and whether an updated inspection is needed. A photograph of shutters does not establish that every opening meets the policy's requirements.

  • Roof: age, permit history, materials, and documented improvements.
  • Inspection: date, inspector qualifications, form version, and any property changes since completion.
  • Protection: documentation for windows, doors, garage doors, and other relevant openings.
  • Quote: confirmation of the information and mitigation credits the insurer actually used.

If you are considering improvements, check the current official My Safe Florida Home program before relying on assistance. Eligibility, funding, application steps, and approval conditions need a current check. Get the insurer's assessment and contractor quotes before treating a potential discount as guaranteed savings.

Where does flood insurance fit into the decision?

Homeowners insurance generally does not cover flood damage. FEMA's FloodSmart buying guide explains separate flood coverage and the NFIP's usual thirty-day waiting period, with exceptions including some mortgage-related purchases. Confirm the applicable exception and effective date for your transaction rather than assuming a policy can always start immediately.

Ask the lender and agent what coverage the specific address requires and what additional coverage you want to evaluate. FloodSmart's eligibility guide explains the requirement for certain federally backed mortgages in high-risk areas. A lender's minimum requirement does not answer every coverage question for the property.

This refresh does not repeat an unverified NFIP authorization deadline. For a time-sensitive closing, check the current FEMA authorization notice and have the agent confirm that the intended policy can be bound with the required effective date. Resolve that operational question early in the purchase timeline.

What should buyers and sellers do before closing?

Buyers should obtain property-specific insurance information while they still have time to resolve it under the contract. Sellers can make that process easier by gathering accurate records before an offer arrives. Neither side should assume a headline about statewide relief settles the cost or insurability of the home.

For a buyer, carry the actual insurance quote into the cash-to-close worksheet and the lender's monthly-payment estimate. Keep premium prepayment and initial escrow funding distinct. Use the Escambia and Santa Rosa property-tax guide for the other major property-specific cost.

For a seller, our selling guide can help organize preparation alongside roof, permit, inspection, and coverage records. A buyer needs their own underwriting and quote; an existing owner's premium is useful context, not a transferable promise.

For both sides, put unresolved insurance questions next to the financing and inspection deadlines. See the buyer planning guide or first-time buyer checklist for the broader sequence. These Florida sources should not be used as Alabama policy rules when comparing homes across the state line.

What should you ask at the next renewal?

Request a comparison you can use, with the same property information and the differences called out. That can reveal an error, a missing document, a coverage choice, or a pricing change that a statewide story cannot explain. Start with these questions and keep the answers with your policy documents.

  1. What changed between my expiring and proposed coverage, premium, and deductibles?
  2. Which property details or mitigation credits are missing or need updated documentation?
  3. What would I pay out of pocket under each quoted hurricane deductible?
  4. Is flood coverage separate, and when would each policy become effective?
  5. What conditions must be satisfied before the replacement coverage is bound?

Have the insurance agent confirm replacement coverage before you cancel an existing policy. If insurance is shaping a move, ask The Costin Team to coordinate the property and closing questions while your licensed insurance professional handles coverage and underwriting. Share this worksheet with the other decision-maker in your household so both of you compare the same costs.

Frequently asked questions

Does the nearly $3 billion insurance decline apply only to Florida homeowners?

No. The original industry-commissioned report combines homeowners and private passenger auto direct written premiums. It presents separate totals for those lines. Those statewide changes do not establish the savings on your particular policy. Compare your renewal with the expiring coverage, premium, deductibles, discounts, and conditions before drawing a household-budget conclusion.

Will every Citizens policy receive the same 2026 decrease?

No. The April implementation bulletin reports statewide averages for specified policy types, with changes varying by product and territory. A homeowners multiperil policy and a wind-only policy also differ in what they cover. Ask your agent to explain the actual proposed renewal and which effective-date rules apply to it.

How much is a percentage hurricane deductible?

Check the dwelling limit and deductible terms on your own policy. In the article’s hypothetical example, a two-percent deductible on four hundred thousand dollars of dwelling coverage is eight thousand dollars. Calculate it from the stated coverage basis, not from the annual premium, and have the agent explain when that deductible applies.

Can a wind-mitigation inspection lower my insurance bill?

Documented qualifying features can affect mitigation credits, but you need the insurer’s evaluation of the property and applicable rules. Ask which features were recognized and whether the inspection remains usable after any changes. Verify the inspector’s qualifications and current form requirements before paying for an inspection or assuming a specific saving.

Does a homeowners policy cover flooding?

Homeowners insurance generally excludes flood damage, so ask about separate coverage for the actual address. The NFIP usually has a waiting period, with exceptions that can matter in a purchase. Have the agent and lender confirm coverage requirements, the applicable exception, and the effective date instead of assuming you can arrange everything at the last minute.

What should I compare before replacing my home insurance?

Review the declarations, covered losses, deductibles, roof settlement provisions, exclusions, endorsements, and any separate flood policy. Confirm that the property information is accurate and that all binding conditions have been satisfied. Have your agent verify the replacement policy and its effective date before canceling the existing coverage.
