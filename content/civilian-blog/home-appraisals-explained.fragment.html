An appraisal is not a second opinion on whether you paid too much. It answers one narrow question for the lender: how much of this price are we willing to finance? When the number lands under the contract price, nothing about the house changed. Your cash requirement did.

That distinction matters because the four ways out of a low appraisal move money between different people, and only one of them is usually worth fighting for.

What does a home appraisal actually decide?

It decides the value your lender underwrites against, and very little else. An appraiser inspects the property, analyzes recent sales and reports an opinion of market value on a standard form. That opinion does not bind you, the seller or the market. It binds the loan.

So the first question is never whether the appraiser is right about the house. It is what the report says and whether the sales behind it hold up.

Who hires the appraiser, and who pays for it?

Your lender orders the appraisal, usually through an appraisal management company, and you pay for it as a loan cost. On the Loan Estimate the appraisal fee appears in Section B, Services You Cannot Shop For, because the lender picks the provider. Compare that whole section between lenders rather than trying to shop the line item, as the CFPB Loan Estimate explainer describes.

The distance is deliberate. Regulation Z prohibits a covered person from causing a dwelling's assigned value to be based on any factor other than the independent judgment of a person that prepares valuations, and it names seeking a minimum or maximum value as an example. Your agent cannot lean on the appraiser, and neither can your lender. See 12 CFR 1026.42, checked September 10, 2026.

You are entitled to read the result. Under Regulation B a creditor must give an applicant a copy of all appraisals and other written valuations developed in connection with an application for credit that is to be secured by a first lien on a dwelling, promptly upon completion, or three business days prior to consummation of the transaction, whichever is earlier. You can waive the timing, so ask for it early instead.

The same section requires written notice of that right not later than the third business day after the creditor receives an application. A creditor shall not charge an applicant for providing a copy of appraisals and other written valuations, although it may still charge you for the appraisal itself. The rule text sits at 12 CFR 1002.14.

Which comparable sales is the appraiser allowed to use?

Fewer than most people assume, and with rules you can check. A minimum of three closed comparables must be reported in the sales comparison approach. Comparable sales that have closed within the last 12 months should be used, though the guide lets an appraiser reach further back when an older sale is genuinely the better indicator and the report explains why.

One line in that same guidance does more work on this coast than the rest of it combined. External factors, including Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) designated flood zone, should be given consideration when selecting comparables, per Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-1.3-08, checked September 10, 2026.

If your Gulf Breeze or Perdido Key home sits in a different FEMA flood zone than the sales used to value it, that belongs in a written reconsideration request rather than a phone call. Insurance cost follows the zone, buyers price it in, and a comp one zone over is not automatically a comp.

What does a low appraisal cost you in cash?

This is a hypothetical illustration, not a quote. Assume a $415,000 contract, a planned 10 percent down payment, and an appraisal that returns $400,000. It assumes the lender sizes the loan against the lower of the two figures, which is exactly what the low number puts to the test. Closing costs and deposits are left out so the gap arithmetic stays visible.

  • The loan you planned on: 90 percent of $415,000 is $373,500, leaving a $41,500 down payment.
  • The loan you now qualify for: 90 percent of $400,000 is $360,000.
  • Cash if the price holds: $415,000 less the loan leaves $55,000 to bring.
  • The real number to argue about: $13,500 of additional cash, which is the $15,000 value gap multiplied by your loan-to-value ratio.

Notice that the extra cash is smaller than the gap itself. People routinely negotiate as though a $15,000 shortfall costs the buyer $15,000. It does not, and knowing the real figure changes what a reasonable split looks like.

How do you request a reconsideration of value?

In writing, with evidence, through your lender's own process. Five federal agencies issued interagency guidance on reconsiderations of value on July 18, 2024, defining it as a request to reassess the value of residential real estate, so most lenders now have a documented procedure you can ask for. The CFPB announcement sets out its scope.

The CFPB describes what a borrower can raise: factual or other errors or omissions, inadequate comparable properties, or evidence that the appraisal was influenced by prohibited bias. Its consumer guidance is the plainest statement of those grounds.

  1. Get the report itself, not your loan officer's summary of it, and read the comparable grid.
  2. Check each comparable for the things the guide names: distance, flood zone, sale date, room count, site size and condition.
  3. Look for factual errors first. A wrong square footage, a missed bathroom or a garage counted as living area is the easiest correction to win.
  4. Assemble two or three better closed sales with addresses, close dates and why each is a fairer match than what the report used.
  5. Send it to the lender in one written package and ask for the reconsideration in writing, then hold the loan timeline while it is reviewed.

Be honest with yourself about the odds. A reconsideration succeeds when the file contains a mistake or a better sale, and it goes nowhere when the only complaint is the number. It also costs days, which matters if your rate lock or closing date is tight.

When will the lender skip the appraisal entirely?

More often than buyers expect, and never on some of the property people here actually buy. Fannie Mae's automated underwriting can return value acceptance, in which case an appraisal is not required for that loan.

The exclusions are where this gets local. Because Fannie Mae lists leasehold properties as ineligible for a value acceptance offer, a Pensacola Beach or Navarre Beach purchase on leased island land should be planned around a full appraisal every time. Our Pensacola Beach leasehold guide covers what else that lease changes.

Two other limits matter on the water. Value acceptance is not offered where the purchase price or estimated value provided to DU is $1,000,000 or more, and a lender may only use one if the value acceptance offer is not more than four months old on the date of the note, per Selling Guide B4-1.4-10. Two- to four-unit properties are excluded as well.

An Orange Beach or Gulf Shores contract at or above $1,000,000 sits outside value acceptance, and the appraiser on that file answers to the Alabama Real Estate Appraisers Board rather than Florida's. In Florida, a certified residential appraiser is a person certified by the department as qualified to issue appraisal reports for residential real property of one to four residential units, under the Real Estate Appraisal Board.

What stalls a coastal condo besides the appraised value?

Reserves, usually. Florida requires a structural integrity reserve study for each condominium building three habitable stories or higher in height, at least every 10 years. In no event may the structural integrity reserve study be completed after December 31, 2026, under section 718.112(2)(g), Florida Statutes.

A study that lands during your contract can produce a special assessment, and an assessment changes both the association's finances and what a lender will do with the project. That is a separate review from the appraisal, and it is worth starting early. Our condo due diligence checklist lists the documents to request first.

What should you do first?

Most low appraisals are settled by arithmetic and a short written package, not by argument. Work through these three steps before anyone starts negotiating.

  1. Request the appraisal report from your lender in writing, and read the comparable grid rather than the summary.
  2. Recalculate the cash on the actual numbers so you are arguing about the real gap instead of the headline difference.
  3. Decide which of the four responses you want before the seller proposes one for you.
