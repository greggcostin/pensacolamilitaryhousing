Start with income, then compare the BAH budget

Your home search needs three useful numbers: a monthly housing scenario using your income and debts, a household scenario with an additional income, and the amount of housing cost you want BAH to cover. Keep them separate. A rank-based BAH table does not know your time in service, monthly debts, credit, insurance quote or intended property.

Use the base pay on your actual Leave and Earnings Statement and discuss which allowances and income the lender can count. The official DFAS pay tables distinguish pay grade and years of service. An assumed base-pay number should not be presented as the verified pay of everyone in that grade.

What does this comparison calculate?

The first scenario is (base pay + BAH multiplied by your selected factor) multiplied by your selected debt ratio, minus other monthly debts. The second adds the separate household income you enter before applying that ratio. The third shows BAH alone. Negative scenario capacity is displayed as zero. These are monthly housing planning figures, not purchase prices, preapprovals or underwriting results.

The default illustration uses $4,000 of monthly base pay, the FL064 E-5 with-dependent BAH of $1,863, a 1.25 BAH factor, a 41% ratio, $500 of other monthly debt and a separate $3,000 additional-income scenario. The $4,000 is a hypothetical input, not an E-5 pay-table claim. Only BAH changes when you choose another grade; replace base pay with your LES amount. The optional gross-up factor affects this ratio illustration, not the cash you receive. Your lender determines what income treatment applies and separately reviews residual income and the rest of the file.

The full housing-cost field is a monthly amount you enter from a property-specific estimate: principal, interest, taxes, homeowners and any flood insurance, plus association dues. The displayed difference is that housing cost minus BAH. Utilities, maintenance, savings and other living expenses still need their own budget lines. None of these fields is sent by the calculator to the contact worker or stored by this calculator.

Which 2026 BAH area should I use?

DoD identifies FL064 as Pensacola and FL056 as Eglin AFB. Eglin, Hurlburt and Duke-area assignments use the Eglin housing area for the standard duty-station examples here. NAS Pensacola, Corry, Saufley and Whiting examples use Pensacola. A standard CONUS entitlement is based on the permanent duty station ZIP, grade and dependency status. Confirm special circumstances and any individual rate protection with your finance office. Use the official DoD lookup.

What changes for a dual-military household?

DoD states that two BAH-eligible service members married with children generally use one with-dependent rate and one without-dependent rate. Do not count the same dependents twice. Use each member's actual LES and confirm exceptions with finance. A spouse's separate military allowance also should not be entered as ordinary taxable salary without discussing its treatment with the lender. DoD's dual-military BAH rule.

For example, two E-5s in FL064 using one with-dependent and one without-dependent rate total $3,507 a month in BAH ($1,863 + $1,644). The equivalent FL056 example totals $4,590 ($2,433 + $2,157). Those are allowance examples only. Separate locations, dependents, government quarters, service status or other circumstances require an individual finance review.

How do I turn the monthly plan into a purchase price?

  1. Obtain a lender scenario. Use a dated rate, term, down payment, funding-fee status and any financed fee. A national conventional mortgage average is not a VA offer.
  2. Use the property's tax estimate. Confirm the county, assessed-value assumptions, exemptions and non-ad valorem charges. Escambia, Santa Rosa, Okaloosa and Baldwin calculations are not interchangeable.
  3. Quote the insurance. Include homeowners, wind and flood coverage as applicable, deductibles and any required inspections. A fixed inland allowance is not a cap on coastal premiums.
  4. Include dues and reserves. Add HOA or condo dues, any known assessment, maintenance and utilities before deciding what feels sustainable.
  5. Compare closing cash and your next move. Keep money available for closing, moving and repairs; test a sale or rental plan if you PCS again.

Use the mortgage calculators for a price-and-payment scenario and the VA purchase guide for entitlement, fees and property requirements. Gregg Costin can help compare the houses and local ownership questions; your lender confirms the loan terms and approval.

How should temporary lodging affect the purchase timeline?

A PCS closing date should leave room for the actual report date, lodging availability, inspections and lender requirements. TLE can partially reimburse qualifying CONUS lodging and meals; it is not a promise that every hotel night will be reimbursed. DoD increased the standard CONUS destination period to 21 days effective November 27, 2024. Eligibility, documentation and any extension still need confirmation. TLF describes lodging facilities, while TLA is a separate allowance commonly associated with overseas moves. DoD's TLE change and current TLE questions.

Ask the installation housing office about current housing options and lodging requirements, then plan the home purchase around a realistic contract timeline. An estimate of temporary lodging coverage should not force you into an unsuitable house.
