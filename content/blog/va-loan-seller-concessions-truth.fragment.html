Here's a scenario I see at least once a month: a military buyer is told by their agent or lender that "the seller can only contribute 4% on a VA loan." They structure the offer accordingly, leaving money on the table.

That advice is wrong. Or more precisely, it's an oversimplification that costs veterans real money.

What the VA Handbook Actually Says

VA Pamphlet 26-7, Chapter 8, Topic 5 draws a clear line between two separate categories:

Seller-Paid Closing Costs (NO CAP): Payment of the buyer's normal, reasonable, and customary closing costs is NOT a seller concession. The seller can pay 100% of your closing costs with no VA rule violation.

Seller Concessions (4% Cap): The 4% limit only applies to concessions — things of value added to the transaction that the seller is not customarily expected to pay.

The Math on a $350,000 Pensacola Purchase: $14,000 in allowable concessions PLUS $7,000 in seller-paid closing costs = $21,000 in total seller contributions. Same VA benefit, $7,000 difference depending on your lender.
