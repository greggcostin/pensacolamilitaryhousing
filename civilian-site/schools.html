About the school data, grades and map

Magnet and charter filters use published directory flags. Programs, addresses and openings can change. Confirm enrollment and attendance zones directly with the district or school.

Christian schools include Catholic and other Christian affiliations explicitly reported by NCES or the school. Affiliation is never inferred from a school's name. CP markers identify documented Christian private schools. P markers identify other private schools, including those with an unknown affiliation. CP and P identify school types, not accountability grades.

Private schools do not receive the public-school accountability letters shown here. Alabama public-school grades and scores come from its 2024–25 release; Florida results use 2025–26. An approved waiver or an absent result remains ungraded. The states use different formulas and should be compared within their own systems.

What should actually matter to parents

A letter grade is one data point, not a verdict. When families ask us how to evaluate a school, we point them to the same checklist every time:

  • Visit in person. Visit the campus and ask how its daily routines, student support and programs would fit your child.
  • Match the school to YOUR child. A school that is right for a self-directed kid may be wrong for one who needs structure, and the reverse. Programs (arts, STEM, athletics, IB/AP, ESE services) matter more than a one-letter summary.
  • Look at the trend, not one year. A school moving from C to B to A is a different story than one drifting the other way. Florida public-school reports show up to three years of available grade history for this reason.
  • Read the components. Two schools with the same letter can have very different achievement profiles. The subject-area percentages show where a school is strong.
  • Confirm zoning before you buy. Attendance zones change. Verify the address you are considering with the district's current zoning tools, not a listing's claim.
  • Context matters. School-level percentages do not predict an individual child's experience. Discuss your child's needs, interests and support with the school.

How records and distances are handled

NCES identities and state district/school identifiers link directory records to available results. Source records stay distinct when a school has more than one identity, while the guide registry preserves a single published guide URL where a duplicate was verified. Recorded campus and approximate Census address points support straight-line distance. Road estimates are requested separately and do not include live traffic. Neither distance nor a map marker establishes attendance eligibility.

Build a school plan alongside the property shortlist

  1. Write down the student's learning priorities, support needs and ordinary weekday routine.
  2. For each property, ask the district about the intended school year and address. Keep a charter or private-school application separate from an assigned-school answer.
  3. Compare actual campus trips, care, activities and sibling schedules. Add tuition or parent-provided transport to the household budget when applicable.
  4. Keep the school's response and any unanswered questions with the home comparison before making a school-dependent decision.

School finder questions, answered.

Which areas does the school finder cover?

The finder contains 274 source records linked to 271 school guides across Escambia, Santa Rosa and Okaloosa counties in Florida, and Baldwin County in Alabama. Search by school name, city, ZIP code or a home address. Coverage follows dated federal directories and documented school additions; it is not a live list of every opening, closure or preschool.

Does the school map show my assigned attendance zone?

No. The map shows school locations and distances, not attendance boundaries or an enrollment assignment. Confirm a specific home address, current school year, grade level and any choice or transfer requirements with the school district. Private schools set their own admissions requirements.

Where do the school grades and directory records come from?

The compiled records include 119 Florida public-school letter grades from 2025-26 and 46 Alabama public-school letter grades from 2024-25. Public directory data use NCES 2024-25; private directory and affiliation data use NCES 2023-24, with separately dated school-published additions and corrections. Compilation and page review dates do not change those source years.

Can I compare Florida and Alabama letter grades directly?

The two states use different accountability formulas and reporting years. Compare results within the same state and year, then read the school guide and original state source. An approved waiver or unpublished result is not a failing grade, and a letter grade does not predict an individual child's experience.

Does the finder include private, Christian, charter and magnet schools?

Yes. The 271 guides include 208 public-school guides and 63 private-school guides. 46 private guides have a documented Christian affiliation and also appear under Private. Charter and magnet filters use recorded designations. CP and P map markers identify school types, not academic grades; unknown affiliations remain unknown.

Are the distances driving distances or live commute times?

The radius filter and nearest-school sorting use straight-line distance from the selected address or ZIP reference point. Calculate drive requests a separate road-route estimate for one selected school. The road miles and estimated duration are not live-traffic predictions, bus routes or proof of an accessible campus entrance.

Can I download the school data or use the guides without the map?

Yes. The source-linked 274-record directory is available as JSON and CSV in the data and methodology section. All 271 school guides have ordinary page links below the map, so the guide directory remains usable without JavaScript. The downloads include source years, source URLs, missing values and shared guide identifiers.

How do I report an incorrect school record?

Use the contact link in the data and methodology section. Include the school name, its guide URL, the field to correct and a current official school or agency source. Source-backed corrections can be checked against the original identity and publication date.
