Your down payment is only part of the cash needed to buy a home. Closing costs also include financing charges, settlement services, government charges and money collected for expenses that arrive after you take possession. Keep those categories separate and the estimate becomes much easier to question.

For an Escambia or Santa Rosa County purchase, start with the signed contract, the lender's estimate and an itemized settlement quote. A generic percentage cannot tell you whether the seller agreed to pay a particular charge or whether the insurance deposit reflects the house you are buying.

What counts as closing costs for a Florida buyer?

Closing costs are the charges and prepaid expenses associated with completing the purchase and loan. They are separate from the down payment. The useful question is which amounts buy a service, which are taxes, and which fund your own future bills.

Which Florida taxes belong in the estimate?

For an ordinary taxable transaction outside Miami-Dade, Florida's deed documentary stamp rate is $0.70 per $100 of consideration or fraction thereof. The recorded mortgage rate is $0.35 per $100 secured or fraction thereof. These use different tax bases, according to the Florida Department of Revenue, checked September 9, 2026.

Florida's nonrecurring intangible tax rate is 0.002 of the obligation secured by Florida real estate, subject to applicable limits and exceptions. The Department of Revenue identifies the lender as liable and allows the charge to be passed to the borrower. Ask the settlement agent to confirm treatment for your transaction.

  • Check consideration. A deed tax calculation is not automatically based on the cash down payment.
  • Check the obligation. Assumptions, refinances, exemptions and multiple collateral properties can require different treatment.
  • Check rounding. Documentary stamp calculations use each taxable hundred dollars or fraction, not simply an unrestricted decimal multiplication.

Who pays title insurance and the other charges locally?

The payment allocation for your purchase belongs in the contract and closing instructions. County custom is not a substitute for those documents. Have the agent and settlement company identify each charge you agreed to pay before comparing your quote with a friend's closing.

For a Pensacola purchase, highlight the owner's title policy, lender's title policy, search and settlement charges separately. Then have the closing company explain the coverage, endorsements and any applicable credits. A bundled title number makes it harder to understand what is included.

For a Pace or Milton purchase, use the same process with the actual Santa Rosa County property and contract. Crossing the county line does not settle who pays a negotiated expense. Ask whether the recording estimate reflects the expected documents and page counts rather than carrying over an allowance from another file.

What does a worked closing-cost example show?

This hypothetical example shows the tax arithmetic on a $350,000 purchase with a $315,000 new mortgage secured entirely by the Florida property. It assumes an ordinary taxable transaction and no exemption. It is not a complete quote or a statement about which party pays.

  • Deed stamps: 3,500 taxable units times $0.70 equals $2,450.
  • Mortgage stamps: 3,150 taxable units times $0.35 equals $1,102.50.
  • Intangible tax: $315,000 times 0.002 equals $630.
  • Combined financing taxes: $1,102.50 plus $630 equals $1,732.50, before lender and settlement charges.

Now add the actual down payment, contracted buyer charges and prepaid amounts. Subtract deposits already credited and allowable seller or lender credits. Leave a separate reserve for moving and immediate repairs. The tax subtotal is useful because it is reproducible; it does not justify presenting a guessed total as an exact cash-to-close figure.

How do you compare two lenders without being misled?

Compare offers with the same loan amount, product, occupancy, rate-lock period and points. The CFPB Loan Estimate explainer helps you separate loan charges from estimates of third-party and property costs. A lower estimated insurance deposit does not prove the lender offers cheaper financing.

For a covered mortgage application, the lender generally must provide a Loan Estimate within three business days of receiving the application. CFPB also explains that receiving the estimate is not loan approval. Read its Loan Estimate guidance and resolve missing assumptions before selecting an offer.

Use the mortgage rate and points comparison to evaluate upfront costs over your expected loan holding period. A lender credit can reduce cash needed now while changing the ongoing cost. Neither choice is automatically better for a buyer expecting a short stay.

What changes for coastal homes and rental purchases?

The property and intended use can change the financing and insurance assumptions, so they need to be disclosed at the start. A furnished rental, condominium and owner-occupied house should each have their own written estimate. Do not copy a primary-home quote into an investment budget.

For a Gulf Breeze coastal property, get an address-specific insurance quote and inspect the deductible alongside the premium. Our insurance checklist identifies the documents to gather. For an Escambia rental, obtain a buyer-based tax estimate rather than adopting the seller's exemption-adjusted bill; use the local property-tax guide.

  • Plan operating cash. Furnishings, utilities, vacancy and repairs sit outside the closing statement.
  • Verify use. Financing approval does not establish rental permission from a local authority or association.
  • Keep jurisdictions separate. A coastal Alabama purchase needs Alabama closing and tax advice, not these Florida tax formulas.

What should you check before wiring the final amount?

For mortgages covered by this rule, the Closing Disclosure must arrive at least three business days before closing. CFPB's Closing Disclosure guidance explains the review period and product exceptions. Use the time to compare the final figures with the estimate.

  1. Confirm loan amount, interest rate, payment and lock status with the lender.
  2. Match seller credits and expense allocations to the signed agreement.
  3. Check earnest money, tax prorations and payments made outside closing.
  4. Ask the settlement company to explain every material change in cash to close.
  5. Confirm transfer instructions through an independently verified contact method before sending funds.

If the amount uses nearly all available savings, revisit the purchase budget before treating the closing date as the only problem.
