# Press Response Templates — Source of Sources / Qwoted / Featured

Purpose: answer journalist source-requests in under 5 minutes with a quotable,
stat-backed response that earns a citation link to a specific deep page (not just
the homepage). Every figure below is already published and source-verified on the
page it cites, so nothing needs re-checking before you paste.

## How to work these platforms (2 minutes of theory)

1. **Speed wins.** Reporters take the first 3-5 usable quotes. Answer within the
   hour when you can; set the platform's email alerts to instant.
2. **Answer the question they asked, in a quotable sentence or two.** They want
   copy they can paste, not an essay. Lead with the stat.
3. **Always include the deep URL** where the number lives. That is the page the
   reporter links. Ask for nothing else.
4. Plain text only. No attachments, no formatting, no sales pitch.
5. Pick requests where a Pensacola/military angle genuinely fits. Skip the rest;
   irrelevant answers get you muted.

## Template A — BAH / military housing affordability

Use for: "How does military housing allowance work?", "Can service members afford
homes in 2026?", cost-of-living and military-market stories.

> Housing allowance is set by duty station, not by where you live, and the spread
> matters more than people think. In 2026, an E-5 with dependents stationed at NAS
> Pensacola receives $1,863 a month, while the same E-5 at Hurlburt Field, less than
> an hour east, receives $2,433 because it falls in the Fort Walton Beach housing
> area. For officers the gap is wider: an O-3 with dependents draws $2,271 in
> Pensacola versus $3,399 in the Fort Walton Beach area. Families who understand
> that difference shop in a completely different price band.
>
> Full 2026 tables by rank for both housing areas:
> https://pensacolamilitaryhousing.com/bah-rates

## Template B — VA loans / funding fee / zero down

Use for: "What should veterans know about VA loans?", first-time-buyer stories,
mortgage-cost comparisons, veteran finance pieces.

> The VA loan is still the strongest mortgage product in America: zero down, no
> monthly mortgage insurance, and with full entitlement there is no loan limit.
> The one cost buyers forget is the funding fee: 2.15% of the loan on first use
> with nothing down, 3.30% on subsequent use, and it drops to 1.25-1.5% with 5-10%
> down. Veterans rated 10% or more disabled, and Purple Heart recipients, pay no
> funding fee at all, which on a $400,000 loan is an $8,600 swing many eligible
> buyers never claim.
>
> Plain-English breakdown and 2026 fee tables:
> https://pensacolamilitaryhousing.com/va-funding-fee-2026
> (or the full guide: https://pensacolamilitaryhousing.com/va-loan-guide)

## Template C — PCS moves / buying remotely / relocation timelines

Use for: "How do military families buy homes during a move?", remote-buying and
relocation-trend stories, summer PCS-season pieces.

> Military families buy remotely more than any other group. Per NAR's 2025 military
> homebuyer data, 41% of active-duty buyers moved more than 500 miles from their
> previous home, and 32% bought in the middle of a job-related relocation. The
> mechanics have caught up: Florida has allowed fully remote online notarization
> since January 2020, a specific power of attorney can close a VA loan while the
> buyer is deployed, and a VA purchase typically closes in 30-45 days. I run live
> video walkthroughs on nearly every PCS purchase; most of my buyers see their
> street for the first time with a moving truck behind them.
>
> The remote-purchase playbook:
> https://pensacolamilitaryhousing.com/buying-sight-unseen-pcs-pensacola
