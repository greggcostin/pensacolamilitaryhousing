How do I choose a realtor in Pensacola?

Check three things. Verified reviews across more than one platform, with enough volume to mean something. Real credentials: negotiation and representation certifications like RENE, ABR, and SRS rather than just a license. And local specifics: ask about flood zones, insurance costs, and neighborhood pricing, then watch whether you get precise answers or generalities.

What questions should I ask before hiring an agent?

Ask how many transactions they closed in the last year, how they will communicate and how often, what their pricing or offer strategy looks like in writing, what happens if you are unhappy, and what they know about insurance and flood zones on the specific streets you are considering. A strong agent enjoys these questions. A weak one changes the subject.

Buying

What does a buyer's agent cost in Pensacola?

Buyer-agent compensation is negotiable and agreed in writing before you tour homes, per the 2024 NAR settlement rules. In many Pensacola transactions the seller offers to cover some or all of it, and where they do not, it becomes part of your offer strategy. You will see every number up front before you sign anything. The full buying process is on the buy page.

What is the first step to buying a home in Pensacola?

A 15-minute conversation about your goals, timeline, and budget, then a lender pre-approval. Pre-approval is free, takes about a day, and tells you your real budget while making your offers credible. Only then does touring homes make sense. Skipping this order is how buyers fall in love with homes they cannot win.

How long does it take to buy a house?

From accepted offer to keys, a financed purchase in the Pensacola area typically runs 30 to 45 days: inspection in the first week or two, appraisal in weeks two to four, then closing. The search before that is entirely up to you. Some clients find the right home in a week, others take six months. Cash purchases can close in under two weeks.

Are there programs for first-time buyers in Florida?

Yes. Florida's Hometown Heroes program offers down-payment help to eligible workers as a deferred, zero-percent second mortgage, not a grant. FHA loans allow 3.5 percent down, conventional programs start at 3 percent, and VA and USDA loans can be zero down for those who qualify. Which one fits depends on your income, credit, and the home's location. We walk through it with your lender.

Should I skip the home inspection to win a bidding war?

No. In this market and this climate, an inspection is how you learn about the roof's real age, the wiring, and the wind mitigation features that set your insurance price. There are safer ways to strengthen an offer: shorter timelines, an appraisal-gap plan, or an inspection for information only. We will never advise going in blind on the Gulf Coast.

Selling

What does it cost to sell a house in Pensacola?

Plan for commission, which is fully negotiable and agreed in your listing paperwork, plus title and closing fees, any agreed buyer concessions, prorated taxes, and whatever prep the home needs. All-in costs commonly land in the seven to nine percent range of the sale price, and you get an exact net sheet before you list, not after. Start with the sell page.

How is my home's list price determined?

With a comparative market analysis built from recent closed sales, active competition, and current buyer demand in your neighborhood, not an automated estimate. Zillow's Zestimate does not walk your street or see your kitchen. You get the data behind the number and a pricing strategy matched to your timeline: speed, top dollar, or balance.

How long do homes take to sell in Pensacola?

It varies by price band, neighborhood, and season, and honest agents say so. Well-priced homes in demand areas still move in days to a few weeks, while overpriced listings sit and go stale. The data changes monthly, so ask for the current days-on-market picture for your specific neighborhood when we run your numbers.

What should I fix before selling?

Only what pays. Paint, lighting, deep cleaning, and curb appeal return more than their cost almost every time. Roof and HVAC issues matter because they hit both the buyer's inspection and their insurance quote. Full kitchen remodels rarely return their cost at sale. We walk the house and give you a short list ranked by return, not a renovation wish list.

Do I have to sell before buying my next home?

Not always. Options include sale contingencies, negotiated leasebacks that let you stay through your next purchase, bridge financing, and in some cases buying first. Which is realistic depends on your equity and the price band you are shopping. This is a strategy conversation, and it is exactly what the first call is for.

The market and the area

Which Pensacola neighborhoods are best?

Best for what is the real question. East Hill for walkable character, Gulf Breeze for schools, Pace and Milton for space per dollar, Navarre for beach access with a small-town feel, Perdido Key for waterfront. Our neighborhoods guide breaks each one down honestly, including the trade-offs.

How bad is home insurance in Pensacola really?

It is a real cost and it varies enormously by roof age, construction year, wind mitigation features, and flood zone, often by thousands of dollars a year between two similar houses. The fix is checking insurability before you fall in love: we flag roof age and flood zones on every showing list and get insurance quotes during the inspection window, never after.

How do property taxes work in Florida?

Florida has no state income tax, and primary residences get a homestead exemption of up to $50,000 off assessed value plus the Save Our Homes cap, which limits assessment growth to 3 percent a year while you own the home. Buyers should note taxes reset to near market value the year after purchase, so the seller's current tax bill understates yours.

Is now a good time to buy or sell in Pensacola?

The honest answer is that it depends on your situation, not the headlines. Rates, inventory, and insurance costs move month to month, and the right answer differs for a growing family, a downsizer, and an investor. What we will not do is tell you it is always a great time. Bring your actual numbers and timeline, and you will get an actual answer.
