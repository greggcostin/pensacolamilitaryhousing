How Property Taxes Actually Work in Escambia and Santa Rosa County

Your TRIM notice is not a bill. Your neighbor's tax bill tells you almost nothing about yours. And there is a property tax amendment on the November ballot. Here is the whole machine in plain English, with the local dates that matter.

August 27, 2026 · By Gregg Costin, Realtor · Reviewed and updated August 2026

Property tax is the line item buyers underestimate most often on the Gulf Coast. Not because the rates are shocking, but because the way Florida calculates the number is genuinely unintuitive. Two identical houses on the same street can carry tax bills that differ by thousands of dollars a year, and both are correct.

TRIM notices went out this month across both counties, so this is the season when the questions arrive. Here is how the system actually works, what the notice in your mailbox is telling you, the deadlines that are running right now, and the change Florida voters will decide in November.

The Formula Everything Reduces To

Strip away the jargon and every Florida property tax bill is the same three-step calculation:

  • The Property Appraiser sets your market value, then applies caps and exemptions to produce a taxable value.
  • The taxing authorities, meaning the county commission, the school board, your city, and various districts, each set a millage rate based on their budgets.
  • Taxable value divided by 1,000, multiplied by total millage, equals your tax.

The Santa Rosa County Property Appraiser puts it the same way on its ad valorem tax page: the tax rate is set by dividing a taxing authority's proposed budget by the total assessed value of all non-exempt property in the district. Two different offices, two different jobs. The Property Appraiser never sets a rate. The county commission never sets your value.

Market Value, Assessed Value, Taxable Value

Your TRIM notice lists three values, and people routinely read the wrong one.

Just or market value is what the Property Appraiser believes the property would sell for. Assessed value is market value after any assessment caps are applied, which is why it is often lower. Taxable value is assessed value minus your exemptions, and it is the only one that gets multiplied by the millage rate.

If your market value jumped this year but your assessed value barely moved, the cap is doing its job. If your market value stayed flat but your taxes rose, look at the millage side of the page instead.

Save Our Homes: Why Your Neighbor's Bill Tells You Nothing

Florida's Save Our Homes provision caps how fast the assessed value of a homesteaded property can rise: the lower of 3 percent or the change in the Consumer Price Index. The Santa Rosa County Property Appraiser reports that the CPI figure for 2026 came in at 2.7 percent, so 2.7 percent is the cap for this tax year.

Compounded over a decade in a rising market, that gap gets enormous. A neighbor who bought in 2012 and homesteaded immediately may be assessed hundreds of thousands of dollars below what the house would sell for today. Their bill is a record of how long they have owned the home. It is not a preview of yours.

The cap resets on a change of ownership. On January 1 following your purchase, the county reassesses at full market value and your own protection starts building from there. This one rule explains most of the surprised phone calls we take in November.

What the Homestead Exemption Is Worth in 2026

If the home is your permanent residence as of January 1, you qualify for the homestead exemption. It has two tiers. The first 25,000 dollars of assessed value is exempt from all property taxes. A second tier applies to value between 50,000 and 75,000 and is exempt from everything except school district taxes.

That second tier used to be a flat 25,000. Constitutional Amendment 5, approved by Florida voters in November 2024, now indexes it to inflation each year. For the 2026 tax year the additional exemption is 26,411 dollars, bringing the maximum combined exemption to 51,411 dollars for a fully qualifying homestead.

Filing is free, it is not automatic when you buy, and the deadline is March 1. We walk through eligibility, portability, and the stacking exemptions in our Florida homestead exemption guide. Veterans with a service connected disability rating should also review the additional exemptions on our military site's VA disability property tax page, because those can be worth considerably more than the standard homestead.

Millage: Who Actually Sets Your Rate

Nobody sets one countywide rate. Your total millage is the sum of every taxing district your parcel happens to sit inside, which is why two homes a mile apart can carry different rates. Both counties publish the full breakdown, and the differences are worth knowing before you shop.

Escambia County

The Escambia County Tax Collector's millage rate detail lists these totals for the 2025 tax year, by the area of the county you live in:

  • Pensacola Beach: 12.7185 mills. County 6.6000, School 5.3590, Library 0.3590, Children's Trust 0.3798, Water Management 0.0207.
  • Unincorporated Escambia County: 13.4035 mills. Same as the beach plus the Sheriff MSTU at 0.6850.
  • Town of Century: 13.6389 mills. County, School, Library, Children's Trust, Water Management, plus town millage of 0.9204.
  • City of Pensacola: 17.0080 mills. The city levies 4.2895 and city residents do not pay the Sheriff MSTU.
  • Downtown Improvement district: 19.0080 mills. City of Pensacola plus the 2.0000 downtown levy.

Worth noting inside that school figure: of the 5.3590 School Board mills, 2.2480 is set by the local board and 3.1110 is required by state law. Only part of your school tax is a local decision.

Santa Rosa County

The Santa Rosa County Property Appraiser's millage table for the 2026 tax year shows County 3.8850, Sheriff 2.0700, School 5.4030, and Water Management 0.0201, which produces these totals:

  • Gulf Breeze: 11.2804 mills. City millage of 1.9723, and no Sheriff levy.
  • Unincorporated Santa Rosa County: 11.3781 mills. This covers Pace, Navarre, Holley, Bagdad, East Milton, Midway, Avalon, Allentown, and the rest of the county.
  • Milton: 13.4261 mills. City millage of 4.1180, and no Sheriff levy.
  • Jay: 16.5481 mills. Town millage of 5.1700 on top of the county and Sheriff levies.

That gap is real money. Unincorporated Santa Rosa at 11.3781 sits about 2.0 mills below unincorporated Escambia at 13.4035, and about 5.6 mills below the City of Pensacola at 17.0080. On a taxable value of 300,000, those two comparisons are worth roughly 607 dollars and roughly 1,689 dollars a year. Same house, same price, different side of a line on a map.

One timing caution. Millage is proposed in August on your TRIM notice and formally adopted at the September budget hearings, so treat any current-year figure as proposed until those hearings finish. Escambia's Tax Collector currently publishes 2025 as its posted year, and Santa Rosa's 2025 unincorporated total was 11.3897, a hair above the 2026 figure.

How to Read the Notice in Your Mailbox

The Notice of Proposed Property Taxes, universally called the TRIM notice, is not a bill. It is a proposal, and it is the most useful document the county sends you all year. Read it in this order:

  • Compare last year's column to this year's. Both values and rates are printed side by side.
  • Check the exemption line. If you bought recently and filed for homestead, confirm it actually appears.
  • Check the property description. Wrong square footage, a pool that no longer exists, or an outbuilding you never had are all fixable errors.
  • Note the public hearing dates. The rate side of your bill is decided in those rooms, and the meetings are open.
  • Find the petition deadline. It is printed on the notice and it is strictly enforced.

The Deadlines Running Right Now

These are the 2026 dates published by the offices themselves, and they differ by county.

Escambia County. The Property Appraiser's 2026 important dates list the Notice of Proposed Taxes as mailed August 14, 2026, with September 8, 2026 as the final date to file a Value Adjustment Board petition for the year.

Santa Rosa County. The Clerk of Court's Value Adjustment Board notice sets the 2026 petition deadline as in office by September 14 at 4:00 p.m. CST, with a filing fee of 50 dollars per petition.

Before you file anything, call the Property Appraiser's office. Genuine errors in square footage, condition, or exemption status are frequently corrected informally, and an informal fix costs nothing. Save the petition for a real disagreement about value, and bring evidence: recent comparable sales, photographs of deferred maintenance, or a contractor's estimate on a structural problem.

Five Things New Buyers Get Wrong

  • Budgeting from the seller's tax bill. The single most expensive assumption in Florida real estate. Budget from your purchase price instead.
  • Assuming homestead transfers with the house. It does not. You file, by March 1, in your own name.
  • Assuming portability is automatic. Moving your Save Our Homes benefit to a new Florida homestead requires a separate application and has its own time limit.
  • Forgetting that the non-homestead cap is different. Second homes, rentals, and investment property are capped at 10 percent a year rather than 3 percent, and that cap does not apply to school district levies at all.
  • Ignoring the year-two escrow shortage. Lenders often set the first year of escrow using the prior owner's tax figure. When the reassessed bill lands, the shortage plus the higher ongoing payment can raise a monthly payment noticeably. Ask your lender to escrow on the reassessed estimate from the start.

What Is on the November 2026 Ballot

Florida voters will decide a constitutional amendment this fall that would substantially change the homestead exemption. According to the Tax Foundation's analysis of the proposal, it would work like this:

  • Raise the homestead exemption on all levies other than school district levies to 150,000 dollars in 2027 and 250,000 dollars in 2028, indexed to inflation after that.
  • Give new Florida residents a 50,000 dollar exemption for their first five years.
  • Tighten the assessment cap on non-homestead property to 5 percent annually beginning in 2027.

Two things to hold onto. First, it requires at least 60 percent voter approval to pass, and nothing changes for the 2026 tax year either way.

Second, school district taxes are excluded, and school millage is a large share of the bill locally: 5.3590 of the 13.4035 mills in unincorporated Escambia County, and 5.4030 of the 11.3781 mills in unincorporated Santa Rosa County. Even full passage would not remove property taxes from a homesteaded bill.

The Tax Foundation also cites legislative estimates of 4.6 billion dollars in reduced revenue in the first year and 8.4 billion in the second. That is why local budget conversations are going to be loud regardless of the outcome.

We are not going to tell you how to vote. We will tell you not to build a purchase budget around a ballot measure that has not passed yet.

A Worked Example

Say you buy a home in unincorporated Escambia County for 350,000 dollars and make it your primary residence. On January 1 following the sale, the assessed value resets toward market value, so start from roughly 350,000 rather than whatever the prior owner was assessed at.

The homestead exemption splits into two calculations, because the second tier does not apply to school taxes:

  • Non-school taxable value: 350,000 minus 51,411 equals 298,589. At the non-school portion of the unincorporated rate, 8.0445 mills, that is about 2,402 dollars.
  • School taxable value: 350,000 minus 25,000 equals 325,000. At the School Board rate of 5.3590 mills, that is about 1,742 dollars.
  • Total: roughly 4,144 dollars a year, or about 345 dollars a month in escrow.

Run the same purchase in unincorporated Santa Rosa County at 11.3781 total mills and the answer is roughly 3,540 dollars, or about 295 dollars a month. That is about 600 dollars a year of difference on an identical 350,000 dollar house, decided entirely by which county line it sits on.

These use published rates for the years noted above, so treat them as close estimates rather than quotes. Pull your actual total from your TRIM notice, because the districts that apply to your specific parcel are what determine it.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my property tax bill higher than the seller's was?

Because Florida resets the clock when a home changes hands. A long-time owner with homestead had years of Save Our Homes protection holding their assessed value below market value. On January 1 after your purchase, the county reassesses the property at full market value and your own exemptions start fresh. The seller's tax bill is a record of their history, not a preview of yours.

Is the TRIM notice a bill I need to pay?

No. TRIM stands for Truth in Millage, and the notice is a proposal. It shows your market value, assessed value, exemptions, taxable value, and the millage rates each taxing authority intends to levy, plus the dates of their public budget hearings. The actual tax bill comes from the Tax Collector in November. The TRIM notice is your window to question the value or attend a hearing.

How do I appeal my assessed value in Escambia or Santa Rosa County?

Start by calling the Property Appraiser's office and asking them to review the parcel, since many errors get corrected informally without a petition. If that does not resolve it, file a petition with the Value Adjustment Board through the Clerk of Court before the deadline printed on your notice. Bring evidence: recent comparable sales, photos of condition issues, or documentation of square footage errors.

What is a mill, and how do I turn millage into dollars?

One mill is one dollar of tax for every one thousand dollars of taxable value. Divide your taxable value by 1,000 and multiply by the total millage that applies to your parcel. Unincorporated Escambia County was 13.4035 mills for 2025, so a 300,000 taxable value there works out to about 4,021 dollars. Your total is the sum of every taxing district your parcel sits inside, so it changes across city and county lines.

Do property taxes go down if home values fall?

Not automatically. Taxing authorities set millage based on their budgets, so if the total assessed value of the county drops, they can raise the millage rate to collect a similar amount. Falling values usually help individual owners only when their own assessment drops relative to their neighbors. Watch both numbers on the TRIM notice, the value and the rate, because either one can move your bill.

Should I budget property taxes based on the listing's tax figure?

No. The tax number shown on a listing reflects the current owner's assessment and exemptions. For budgeting, estimate the taxable value as the price you are paying, subtract the homestead exemption if the home will be your primary residence, and apply the millage rate for that parcel. Lenders often escrow using the old figure in year one, which is why escrow shortages surprise buyers in year two.
