VA Certificate of Eligibility (COE): How Pensacola Military Families Get One in 24 Hours

The COE is the single document that unlocks your VA loan benefit. Most veterans either do not know what it is, cannot find it, or assume it takes weeks, when in reality your lender can pull most COEs in under 5 minutes through the VA's WebLGY system. This page walks you through who qualifies, the three ways to get yours, and what to do when something on the COE is wrong.

Gregg Costin
Retired USAF Combat Systems Officer · Realtor at Levin Rinke Realty (FL & AL) · MRP · ABR · RENE
Reviewed & updated · July 2026

About a third of the military families I work with show up to our first meeting saying "I'm not sure if I'm VA-eligible. I haven't gotten anything from the VA in years." Almost all of them are. Eligibility doesn't expire.

The COE is just the paperwork that proves it, and the VA has gotten dramatically faster at issuing it. If you served at least 90 continuous days of active duty (or 6 years in the Selected Reserve), there is a near-certainty you can get a COE this week and start house-hunting at a Pensacola PCS pace.

What the COE Actually Is

The Certificate of Eligibility is a one-page document the VA issues to confirm you are eligible to use a VA-guaranteed home loan. It states four things:

  • Your eligibility category (Active Duty, Veteran, Reserve/Guard, or Surviving Spouse) and the qualifying service period.
  • Your basic entitlement: the dollar amount the VA will guarantee against your loan if you default. Base entitlement is $36,000; bonus entitlement scales with the conforming loan limit.
  • Your remaining entitlement if you have used a VA loan before.
  • Funding fee status: whether you are exempt (10%+ service-connected disability, surviving spouse receiving DIC, Purple Heart on active duty) or which tier you fall into.

Every VA-approved lender will require it during pre-approval. Without it, the lender cannot start a VA loan file. With it, you are essentially clear to write VA-financed offers as soon as you have a pre-approval letter in hand.

Who Qualifies: Eligibility by Service Type

Eligibility rules differ by component. The VA's full list runs longer than this, but these are the categories that cover 98% of the military families coming through Pensacola:

CategoryMinimum Service Required
Active Duty (currently serving)90 continuous days of active service
Veteran (separated/retired): wartime90 days of active duty during a qualifying wartime period (covers most service since 1990)
Veteran: peacetime181 continuous days of active duty
Veteran: post-1980 enlisted / post-1981 officer24 continuous months of active duty or the full obligation period (typically 36 months)
National Guard / Reserve6 years in the Selected Reserve or 90 days of federal active service (Title 10, not state Title 32)
Surviving SpouseSpouse died on active duty, from a service-connected disability, or is missing/POW

Discharge characterization matters: Honorable and General (Under Honorable Conditions) qualify. Other Than Honorable requires VA case-by-case review. Bad Conduct and Dishonorable generally disqualify.

If you served in a less common path (service academy cadet, ROTC scholarship, certain pre-1980 service, or merchant marine in WWII), you still likely qualify but the documentation process differs. Your lender or the VA Regional Loan Center can confirm.

The Three Ways to Get Your COE (Fastest to Slowest)

Method 1: Through your lender via WebLGY (5 minutes, recommended)

Every VA-approved lender has access to the VA's WebLGY system (Web Loan Guaranty), which queries the VA's eligibility database in real time. For most veterans whose service records are fully digitized (anyone separated since roughly the mid-1990s, plus most active duty and recent Reserve members), the lender pulls the COE the moment they start your file.

This is the path I recommend for every Pensacola military family I work with. By the time we have your pre-approval letter ready to write an offer, the COE is already attached to your loan file. You never see the form, never request it yourself: your lender handles it.

Method 2: VA.gov self-service portal (under 24 hours, sometimes instant)

If you want the COE in hand before talking to a lender (useful for budgeting or to confirm entitlement before house-hunting), request it yourself at va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/how-to-request-coe. Sign in with your VA.gov credentials (or create them with ID.me / Login.gov). For most users, the COE is issued and downloadable within minutes; complex cases route to a human and return in 3-10 business days.

Method 3: Mailed Form 26-1880 (4-6 weeks, last resort)

If you prefer paper, or if your service is missing from the digital system, download VA Form 26-1880, fill it out, attach your DD-214 or equivalent service documentation, and mail to the Atlanta VA Regional Loan Center. This is the slowest path and only worth using if the digital paths fail. Plan on 4-6 weeks for a response.

What Documents You Need

Different categories require different proof. Have these ready before you call your lender or open the VA.gov portal:

  • Active Duty: A current Statement of Service signed by your unit commander or personnel office, on official letterhead. Must include full name, SSN, date of birth, entry date, and time lost (if any).
  • Veterans: DD Form 214, member copy 4 (the version that shows character of service). Member copy 1 will not work.
  • National Guard / Reserve (with no federal active service): Six-year Selected Reserve membership letter signed by the unit's adjutant or personnel officer, plus an honorable discharge certificate if separated.
  • National Guard / Reserve (with federal active service): DD Form 214 from each period of Title 10 active duty, plus current Reserve status letter.
  • Surviving Spouse: VA Form 26-1817 (Request for Determination of Loan Guaranty Eligibility - Unmarried Surviving Spouses), the service member's DD-214, and a marriage certificate. If you have not already established VA dependency, a death certificate showing service-connected cause is also needed.
  • If you have a VA disability rating: your most recent VA award letter showing the rating percentage. This is what triggers the funding fee waiver on the COE.

Understanding Your Entitlement on the COE

Entitlement is the VA's promise to repay your lender if you default, and how much they promise determines how big a loan you can get with zero down. There are two pieces:

  • Basic entitlement: $36,000. Hasn't changed in decades. Covers 25% of a small loan.
  • Bonus entitlement: scales with the conforming loan limit. For 2026, the FHFA conforming limit is $832,750. The VA guarantees up to 25% of that, so bonus entitlement is approximately $208,188. Combined with basic, this lets you borrow up to roughly the conforming limit with zero down.

If you have full VA entitlement, county loan limits no longer apply at all: the Blue Water Navy Act removed them in 2020, so full entitlement covers the entire purchase price with zero down at any price.

The 2026 baseline loan limit for Escambia, Santa Rosa, Okaloosa, and Walton counties is $832,750, and it only matters if you have reduced entitlement from a prior VA loan; in that case the VA guarantees up to 25% of the limit and a lender may ask for a down payment on the amount above it.

If your COE shows reduced entitlement and you have not used a VA loan before, that's a flag: usually means a previous loan was paid off but the entitlement was never restored. See the next section.

Restoring Entitlement After a Prior VA Loan

You get the entitlement back when one of these happens:

  1. Loan paid off in full and the property sold. Restoration is automatic: the lender reports the payoff to the VA and entitlement returns within 30-60 days. No paperwork from you.
  2. Loan paid off but you still own the property (e.g., refinanced into conventional). You can use the one-time entitlement restoration: file Form 26-1880 with the payoff documentation. This is a once-per-lifetime move, so use it strategically.
  3. Loan assumed by a VA-eligible buyer with substitution of entitlement. The buyer's entitlement substitutes for yours, releasing yours fully. The buyer must be VA-eligible and must apply with the lender or VA. See my assumable VA loans guide for the mechanics. This is increasingly valuable in a high-rate market when the seller has a 2.75% loan to pass on.
  4. Compromise sale or short sale. If the VA paid a claim against your default, restoration requires repaying the VA the loss amount.

Special Cases

Surviving Spouses: Don't Skip the DIC Question

If you are receiving Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) from the VA, you are exempt from the funding fee. Verify the COE shows the exemption before your lender prices the loan. I have seen lenders charge the full 2.15% fee on a surviving-spouse purchase because the COE the lender pulled was an old version that did not reflect DIC eligibility. Refresh the COE if anything has changed.

Reserve / Guard with Mixed Service

If you have multiple periods of Title 10 federal active duty plus continuing Selected Reserve membership, your COE should show the cumulative qualifying service. The VA sometimes only pulls the most recent active period. If your COE undercounts your qualifying time and that affects funding fee tier (first-use vs subsequent-use), provide all DD-214s to your lender for a recalculation.

Service Academy and ROTC

Time at West Point, USNA, USAFA, USCGA, USMMA, or USUHS counts toward eligibility, but the documentation is non-standard. Same for ROTC scholarship recipients with subsequent active-duty obligations. The VA will work with you, but plan for the slower mailed Form 26-1880 path with your academy/ROTC service records attached.

Common COE Issues and How to Fix Them

"Eligibility cannot be determined"

Usually means the VA's database does not see your service. Submit a Statement of Service (Active Duty) or your DD-214 (Veteran) through the VA.gov portal under "Submit a request for COE update." Resolution takes 1-3 weeks.

COE shows the wrong funding fee tier

The COE may show "first use" when you have actually used VA financing before, or "subsequent use" when your prior entitlement should have restored. Provide your lender with documentation of the prior loan's status (payoff letter, assumption documentation, or new property deed). The lender can request a corrected COE, usually within a week.

Disability rating not reflected

If you received a VA disability rating after your last COE was pulled, the funding fee waiver will not be on the COE. Upload your most recent VA award letter through VA.gov and ask for COE re-issue. Critical to do before close: the funding fee on a $400K loan at 2.15% is $8,600.

Service is genuinely missing from VA records

Pre-1980 service, certain Reserve gaps, and some special-category service can be missing from the VA's digital records. The National Personnel Records Center (NPRC) in St. Louis is the authoritative source: request your military records via archives.gov, then forward to the VA. This path takes 6-12 weeks. Worth it if you are otherwise eligible.

How the COE Fits into the Pensacola PCS Timeline

If you are PCSing to NAS Pensacola, Whiting Field, Corry Station, Hurlburt Field, or Eglin AFB and want to buy on arrival, here is when the COE happens:

  1. 60-90 days before report date: Pull your COE through your VA-experienced lender as part of pre-approval. If you are dual-military or have a complex service history, give the lender a head start by requesting the COE yourself via VA.gov first.
  2. 30-60 days out: Lender finalizes pre-approval letter using the COE. You can now write VA-financed offers.
  3. Under contract: VA appraisal ordered, COE referenced in the loan file. Funding fee tier confirmed by the COE.
  4. Closing: COE remains in the file as proof of eligibility. You do not need to do anything further with it.

The COE is the single most leverage-able document for getting a VA pre-approval done fast, and pre-approval is what lets you write a competitive offer in the Pensacola spring/summer PCS market when 60-70% of inventory turns over in 90 days.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a VA Certificate of Eligibility (COE)?

The COE is the single document the VA issues to confirm you qualify to use a VA-guaranteed home loan. It states your eligibility category (Active Duty, Veteran, Reserve/Guard, or Surviving Spouse), your remaining entitlement amount, and any conditions like funding fee exemptions for 10%+ service-connected disability. No COE means no VA loan. Every VA-approved lender requires it during pre-approval.

How long does it take to get a VA COE?

Almost instant if your lender pulls it via the VA's WebLGY system: under 5 minutes for the majority of veterans whose service records are fully digitized. The VA.gov self-service portal returns most COEs in under 24 hours. The slowest path, mailing Form 26-1880, takes 4-6 weeks.

Who qualifies for a VA COE?

Active Duty (90 continuous days of active service), Veterans (24 continuous months of active duty or full obligation period with honorable discharge in most cases), National Guard and Reserve members (90 days of federal active service OR 6 years in the Selected Reserve), and surviving spouses of service members who died on active duty or from a service-connected condition. Cadets at the service academies and ROTC also qualify with specific conditions.

What documents do I need to apply for a COE?

It depends on your status. Active Duty: a Statement of Service signed by your unit commander or personnel officer. Veterans: DD Form 214 (member copy 4 with character of service shown). National Guard/Reserve: NGB Form 22 plus an honorable discharge or current Selected Reserve membership letter. Surviving Spouse: VA Form 26-1817 plus the service member's DD-214 and a marriage certificate. If you have a service-connected disability rating, also have your VA award letter handy. It is what waives the funding fee.

Can my lender get the COE for me?

Yes, and they should. Any VA-approved lender (which is most retail and military-focused mortgage lenders) has WebLGY access and can pull your COE in real time during pre-approval. The lender route is the fastest and the path I recommend for every Pensacola military family I work with: by the time we have a pre-approval letter to write an offer, the COE is already in the lender's file.

What is VA entitlement and how does the COE show it?

Entitlement is the dollar amount the VA will guarantee against your loan if you default. Your COE shows your basic entitlement ($36,000 base) and your remaining bonus entitlement (25% of the conforming loan limit, $208,188 in 2026). With full entitlement, county loan limits do not apply and you can buy at any price with zero down. The 2026 baseline limit for Escambia, Santa Rosa, Okaloosa, and Walton counties ($832,750) only caps the zero-down amount if you have reduced entitlement from a previous VA loan that is still active, until that loan is paid off or the entitlement is restored.

How do I restore VA entitlement after a previous VA loan?

Three paths. (1) Pay off the prior VA loan in full: entitlement restores automatically when the lender reports the payoff to the VA, usually within 30-60 days. (2) Sell the property and have the buyer assume your VA loan with substitution of entitlement. The buyer must be VA-eligible. (3) Use the one-time entitlement restoration: if you paid off a VA loan and still own the home (e.g., refinanced into conventional), you can restore once per lifetime via Form 26-1880 with the payoff documentation.

Does my COE expire?

Technically no. The COE itself does not have an expiration date. But practically, lenders require a COE that reflects your current entitlement and any service-connected disability rating changes. If your status has changed since your last COE was issued (you re-enlisted, separated, received a new disability rating, or paid off a VA loan), pull a fresh one before pre-approval.

Can a surviving spouse get a COE?

Yes. Surviving spouses of service members who died on active duty or from a service-connected disability are eligible. File VA Form 26-1817 (Request for Determination of Loan Guaranty Eligibility - Unmarried Surviving Spouses) along with the service member's DD-214 and a marriage certificate. Surviving spouses receiving Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) are exempt from the VA funding fee. Verify this is reflected on the COE so your lender does not charge it.

What if my COE is wrong or incomplete?

Most issues are fixable in 1-2 weeks. Missing service periods get corrected by submitting a Statement of Service or additional DD-214s through the VA.gov portal. Missing disability rating means the funding fee is shown without exemption. Fix by uploading your VA award letter. If your service is genuinely missing from the VA's records (rare, usually pre-1980 service or certain Reserve gaps), the National Personnel Records Center in St. Louis can verify and the VA will reissue. Your lender or a regional VA loan center can advocate on your behalf.
