What should you do 90 days before reporting to Pensacola?

Three things, in this order: get pre-approved with a VA-experienced lender, book household goods counseling with your losing installation's Personal Property Office, and start watching live listings so you learn this market before you have to act in it. At 90 days you have options. At 30 days you have pressure, and in real estate, pressure is expensive.

Pre-approval comes first because it defines everything downstream: your price band, your BAH math, and how seriously listing agents take your offer. Get a true underwritten pre-approval, not a five-minute prequalification, from a lender who closes VA loans every week. My VA loan field manual covers what to ask and what a Pensacola-savvy lender should already know, like local VA appraisal turn times.

Second, the household goods system changed this year. After the HomeSafe contract collapse, the Pentagon stood up the Personal Property Activity on May 1, 2026 to run military moves permanently, and bookings again flow through your installation PPO and the traditional mover network. I wrote a plain-English breakdown of what the PPA means for your 2026 move; the short version is that summer capacity on the Gulf Coast is finite, so the earlier you counsel, the better your pickup window.

Third, set up a saved search on the live MLS search by base and just watch it for two weeks. You will learn more about what $350K buys in Pace versus East Hill by watching 20 listings hit and go pending than from any national real estate site. Then work through the rest of the PCS checklist so nothing ambushes you later.

Here is the pattern I see every spring: the families who call me at 90 days tour calmly by video, write one strong offer, and close a week after arrival. The families who call at 28 days are choosing between the three least-loved houses on the market during peak season. Same BAH, very different outcomes.

Which base determines your BAH: FL064 or FL023?

Your BAH follows your duty station's Military Housing Area, not the town you sleep in. NAS Pensacola, Corry Station, Saufley Field, and NAS Whiting Field all sit in MHA FL064 (Pensacola). Eglin AFB and Hurlburt Field sit in MHA FL023 (Fort Walton Beach), which pays meaningfully more because of Emerald Coast housing costs. An E-6 with dependents gets $2,235 in FL064 versus $2,526 in FL023 in 2026.

That distinction trips people up on orders to Whiting Field: the base is in Milton, 30 miles northeast of Pensacola, but it draws the same FL064 rates as NAS Pensacola. How do you house hunt for Pensacola from another duty station?

Mostly by video, and it works better than people expect. Between 60 and 45 days out, my remote clients tour homes by live video walkthrough, we layer sight-unseen protections into the contract, and many spend up to 10 days of permissive TDY on one focused in-person trip. You do not need to be in Pensacola to buy well in Pensacola.

A real remote tour is not a highlight reel. When I walk a house on video for a client, the flood zone gets pulled before I ever leave the office, and on site we cover the roof line, the electrical panel, the water heater's date sticker, backyard drainage, and what the street sounds like, not just the staged kitchen. If a house only looks good from the angles the listing photos chose, you need to know that from 1,000 miles away, not at the final walkthrough.

On the contract side, buying without setting foot in the house is routine in a military town when it is structured right: full inspection contingency, the VA appraisal backstopping value, and a documented video walkthrough before closing. The complete checklist, including what I refuse to let remote clients waive, is in the sight-unseen buying guide.

House-hunting leave is the third tool. Most services authorize up to 10 days of permissive TDY for house hunting on a CONUS PCS: non-chargeable, but with no travel pay or per diem, and always subject to command approval. The highest-leverage way to use it is not wandering open houses on arrival; it is one surgical trip at 45-60 days out to tour the shortlist you built remotely, sign a contract, and fly back. That sequencing is exactly what lets you close a few days after you report instead of three weeks into a hotel stay.

How do you schedule closing against your report date?

Set closing for five to ten days after you physically arrive, never on or before your report date. A VA purchase typically needs 30-45 days from contract to closing, so a contract signed around 60 days out leaves margin. Temporary lodging exists to bridge the gap between arrival and keys; use it, because stacking your report date, your closing, and your household goods delivery on the same week is how PCS horror stories start.

The reasoning is simple: of the three dates in play, your report date is the only one that cannot move. Appraisals slip. Underwriters ask for one more document. And with the household goods system still settling under the new Personal Property Activity, pickup and delivery windows deserve extra slack this year. My VA closings in 2025-26 have mostly run 30 to 40 days from contract to keys, but I schedule every one as if it will take 45, and nobody has ever complained about closing early.

Two coordination points matter more than buyers expect. First, the rate lock: a standard lock runs 30-45 days, so locking at contract usually covers you, but tell your lender the report date up front so they can pad the lock instead of paying for an extension later. Second, the final walkthrough: if you are still en route, we do it by live video the day before closing, and Florida allows fully remote online notarization, so I have closed buyers who signed from a hotel in Texas mid-drive. Your physical location on closing day is a logistics detail, not an obstacle.

How do TLE and lodging work during arrival week?

Temporary Lodging Expense reimburses up to 21 days of lodging and meals for a CONUS-to-CONUS PCS, capped at $290 per day, under the Joint Travel Regulations change that took effect November 27, 2024 (Defense Travel Management Office). You can split the 21 days between your losing and gaining stations however you like, but you cannot claim TLE for the same days you draw PCS travel per diem.

Practical sequencing: most families burn a few TLE days at the losing station during pack-out, then bank the rest for the Pensacola end while they wait for closing or household goods. In designated housing-shortage areas, TLE can extend to 60 days, but that applies only in specific MHAs on the DoD's list, so confirm your situation with your PPO rather than planning on it. Book lodging the day your orders drop if you are moving in summer; Pensacola's peak PCS season and its tourist season are the same season, and both on-base and off-base rooms tighten. The full rundown of Navy Gateway Inns and Suites, base options, and TLE-friendly off-base stays is on my military lodging and TLE page.

One arrival-week detail nobody briefs you on: which gate to use. NAS Pensacola's gate rules for visitors, DBIDS, and rush-hour flow are their own small project, and showing up to the wrong gate with a moving truck behind you is a bad first morning. I keep a current NAS Pensacola gate guide for exactly this reason; read it before your first Monday, not during it.

How do you enroll kids in Pensacola schools mid-PCS?

Under the Interstate Compact on Educational Opportunity for Military Children, adopted by all 50 states plus DC and DoDEA (Military OneSource), the receiving district must enroll your child immediately using unofficial records, like a hand-carried report card, and place them in comparable courses and programs, including gifted, AP, and career-technical tracks. Escambia and Santa Rosa County districts both operate under it. Enroll from temporary lodging using your orders; do not wait for closing day.

The two-district geography is the local decision that matters. Santa Rosa County (Pace, Milton, Gulf Breeze) earned an A district grade from the Florida DOE for 2024-25, its fourth straight, while Escambia County (Pensacola proper, NAS Pensacola's side of the bay) earned a B. There are excellent individual schools in both, but the county line is a real boundary for a lot of my buyers, and it is one of the first filters I set on a home search when kids are involved. Note the compact covers public schools only.

Timing: both districts start back in the first half of August, so a summer PCS should have enrollment moving before your household goods arrive; check each district's current calendar for exact dates. Each installation also has a School Liaison Officer whose entire job is smoothing this transfer, and they are worth a call before you even pick a neighborhood.

What should your first 90 days in Pensacola look like?

Unpack, document, and file. Log every scratch and missing box at household goods delivery, because you have 180 days from delivery to submit your notice of loss or damage under DoD claims rules. If you bought, file for the Florida homestead exemption. If you rented on a short training pipeline, put a decision date on the calendar now for what happens at your next set of orders.

The homestead exemption is the one with real money attached: it exempts up to $50,000 of your home's assessed value from property taxes and caps future assessment growth, and the filing deadline is March 1 of the year you claim it (Florida Department of Revenue). Military owners have some specific wrinkles worth knowing, including how deployments and out-of-state duty interact with the residency requirement, and I cover them in my homestead exemption guide for military owners.

Beyond paperwork, the first 90 days are when you pressure-test the choice you made from a distance: the commute at actual gate hours, the school drop-off line, the sound of Blue Angels practice if you landed near the flight line (most of us consider that a feature). If you rented, this is when you quietly start watching the market for the buy you might make at the 12-month mark. And if something about the house or neighborhood is not working, tell me early; fixing a housing mistake at month 3 is far cheaper than at month 33.
