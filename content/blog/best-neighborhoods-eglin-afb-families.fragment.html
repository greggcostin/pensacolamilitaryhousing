Which Eglin gate do you work at?

Answer this before you tour a single house. Eglin's East Gate serves Niceville, Valparaiso, and Bluewater Bay via SR-85 and John Sims Parkway; the West Gate serves Fort Walton Beach and Shalimar via Eglin Boulevard and SR-189. Crestview commuters come down SR-85 to the north side of the base, and Duke Field sits closer to Crestview than to anywhere else. Your gate can swing your daily commute by 20 minutes each way.

When I ask a new Eglin client where they'll actually badge in every morning, about half don't know yet. Get that answer from your sponsor before you commit to a town. A 33rd Fighter Wing or 96th Test Wing family working the main base has a genuinely easy life in Niceville. A 7th Special Forces Group family working the northern part of the range would be volunteering for a pointless drive by living in Niceville when Crestview puts them 10-15 minutes from work at two-thirds the price. And AFRL or 96th folks on the west side of the base often do better in Fort Walton Beach or Shalimar than anywhere else. Rush hour is real at both main gates: I plan for a 10-15 minute ID-check line at the East Gate at peak, and SR-85 southbound out of Crestview is a different animal at 0715 than it is at 0530.

Is Niceville the best neighborhood for Eglin AFB families?

For families with school-age kids and the budget to clear it, yes. Niceville pairs a 10-minute East Gate commute with the strongest school reputation in an already strong county, and it has been the default landing spot for Eglin officers and senior NCOs for decades. The catch is the price of admission: Zillow's typical home value for Niceville was $443,421 in mid-2026, up 1.6% year over year, and Redfin's median sale price ran $489,707 in May 2026.

Who it fits: dual-income households, O-3 and above, senior enlisted with some equity from a prior duty station, and anyone who plans to hold the house past one tour. Bluewater Bay is the master-planned heart of the market, with a marina and golf on Choctawhatchee Bay; Rocky Bayou runs quieter and more established; and newer construction is concentrated in long-build-out developments on the east side of town. Okaloosa County schools earned an A district grade from the Florida DOE again for 2025-26, and the district is one of only three in Florida to earn an A every single year since 2014. I deliberately don't quote individual campus grades here because they move annually; check the current FLDOE school grades for the campuses you'd zone into, and my schools-by-base guide maps the zones.

The trade-offs, honestly: you're paying a $100K-$150K premium over Crestview for comparable square footage, inventory under $400K is thin and moves fast, and the Mid-Bay Bridge shortcut toward Destin and Hurlburt is a toll bridge. If you're deciding between the two towns specifically, my Niceville vs Crestview comparison goes street-level.

Is Crestview worth the longer commute to Eglin?

If budget is the deciding factor, yes. Crestview is the cheapest full-service town in the Eglin orbit: Zillow's typical home value was roughly $296K as of July 2026, and Redfin's recent sold median came in near $295K, up 6.3% year over year. In exchange you accept 25-30 minutes down SR-85 to the main base. That is the whole trade, and thousands of Eglin families take it every year.

Crestview is also the new-construction capital of Okaloosa County. NewHomeSource counted 35 active new-home communities from 12 builders in the Crestview area in 2026, with D.R. Horton alone running multiple subdivisions (Independence, Shoal River Landing, and Yellow River Plantation among them). That inventory is why a 4-bedroom new build near $300K is still a realistic target here when it has vanished everywhere south of the range. Builder incentives on rate buydowns have been some of the best financing deals I've seen in 2026; pair them with a zero-down VA loan and your cash-to-close can be startlingly small.

Who it fits: E-4 to E-7 families stretching BAH, 7th SFG and Duke Field personnel (Duke is only 10-15 minutes up SR-85), and anyone who wants land, because the acreage properties toward Laurel Hill and Holt have no Niceville equivalent. The honest downsides: the commute compounds daily, south-side growth has outrun the road network (the PJ Adams corridor at school drop-off hour will test your patience while the bypass work grinds on), and resale competes against a constant stream of brand-new homes. Is Fort Walton Beach a good place to live for Eglin AFB families?

Yes, and it's the most underrated of the three. Fort Walton Beach puts you 5-15 minutes from the West Gate via Eglin Boulevard and SR-189, minutes from the beach, and in the middle of the area's restaurants and services, all at a midpoint price: Zillow's average home value was about $336K in 2026, up 1.5% year over year. One local brokerage market report put the single-family sold median near $374K in June 2026, which tells you the city-wide Zillow number is pulled down by condos and smaller older homes.

Who it fits: West Gate commuters, families who want walkable errands and the Sound-side lifestyle over a master-planned subdivision, and buyers who like character homes. The housing stock is the story here: FWB built out in the 1960s-1990s, so you're often comparing a renovated mid-century house on a big lot against new vinyl construction in Crestview at a similar price. Same Okaloosa A-rated district as Niceville at the district level, but campus-to-campus variability is wider here than anywhere else in the county, so shop the specific school zone, not the town.

The trade-offs: older roofs, plumbing, and electrical mean inspections matter more (and insurance quotes swing hard on roof age; get a real quote before you offer, per my Florida insurance guide). Flood zones near Santa Rosa Sound, Cinco Bayou, and Garnier's Bayou need checking on every listing, and summer beach traffic on US-98 is a fact of life.

What about Shalimar and Navarre?

Shalimar is the sleeper pick and Navarre is the beach-lifestyle pick, but each fits a narrower situation. Shalimar sits roughly 2 miles from Eglin's West Gate, a town literally platted for Eglin officers in the 1940s, with bayou-front streets and the Choctawhatchee High zone. Zillow's average value there ran about $394K in 2026, up 1.0%. The constraint is supply: it's a small town, some months there's barely a dozen listings, and the best waterfront streets rarely trade. When the right Shalimar house lists, it does not wait for your house-hunting trip.

Navarre is a different calculation. Zillow's average value was about $418K in 2026 (Redfin's sold median ran $421K in March 2026, down 7.5% year over year, so buyers have gained leverage there). You get the beach, and you get Santa Rosa County schools, an A district for 2025-26 with A grades every year since 2010. But Navarre is 35-40 minutes from the Eglin main base on a good day. I steer Eglin main-base families away from it unless one spouse works toward Hurlburt, because Navarre-to-Hurlburt is a reasonable 15-20 minutes. If that's your split, my Gulf Breeze vs Navarre comparison is the next read. Destin deserves one honest line too: gorgeous, 20-25 minutes out, and at Destin prices it's mostly an O-4-and-up or dual-income play.

Can you afford these towns on Eglin's 2026 BAH?

Mostly yes, but the margins differ by town and pay grade, so run the actual numbers. Eglin sits in the FL023 Fort Walton Beach MHA, where 2026 with-dependents BAH runs $2,340 for E-1 to E-4, $2,433 for E-5, $2,526 for E-6, $2,841 for E-7, $3,399 for O-3, and $3,528 for O-4 (DoD 2026 tables, up 0.4% from 2025). The full grid, including without-dependents rates, lives on my BAH rates page.

Here's the math at the 6.69% average 30-year fixed from the week of August 6, 2026 (Freddie Mac PMMS), assuming a zero-down VA purchase at each town's typical value. Principal and interest only: Crestview at $296K runs about $1,908 a month; Fort Walton Beach at $336K about $2,166; Shalimar at $394K about $2,540; Navarre at $418K about $2,695; Niceville at $443K about $2,856. Now subtract from BAH: an E-5 in Crestview keeps roughly $525 a month for taxes and insurance, which usually works. An E-6 in FWB keeps about $360, which is tight once you see a real Florida insurance quote. An O-3 in Niceville at the typical value keeps about $543, and an O-4 about $672; workable, but a $490K Redfin-median purchase instead of a $443K typical one erases that cushion entirely.

Three honest notes. First, these figures exclude property taxes, insurance, and the VA funding fee, and Gulf Coast insurance is the line item that breaks budgets; quote it per-house, early. Second, VA rates often price slightly below the conventional survey average, which helps. Third, BAH-sized affordability is a floor question, not a ceiling permission slip: my BAH-to-mortgage guide walks through how lenders actually count your allowance, and the on-base comparison linked above puts the same math against privatized housing.

How should you actually decide?

Use this order of operations: gate first, schools second, budget third, lifestyle last. It sounds cold, but it's how families end up happy at month eighteen instead of resentful at month three. Confirm where you badge in. Pull FLDOE grades for the specific campuses, not the district. Run your BAH against a real payment, not a Zillow estimate. Then, and only then, argue about waterfront versus new-build.

The shorthand I give clients: main-base with kids and the budget, Niceville. Budget-first, new construction, Duke Field or 7th Group, Crestview. West Gate, character homes, beach errands, Fort Walton Beach or Shalimar if inventory cooperates. Hurlburt in the mix, Navarre.
