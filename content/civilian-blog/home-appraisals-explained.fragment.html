An appraisal provides an opinion of a property's market value that the lender uses in its financing decision. If that opinion comes in below your contract price, the lender may reduce the proposed loan. Before offering more cash or asking the seller for a reduction, read the report and get the revised loan figures.

The four responses below affect cash, timing and negotiating positions differently. Your contract, available reserves and the evidence behind the appraiser's conclusion determine which response is workable.

What does a home appraisal actually decide?

It supplies the value your lender uses when evaluating the property's collateral. An appraiser inspects the property, analyzes recent sales and reports an opinion of market value on a standard form. That opinion does not bind you, the seller or the market. The lender evaluates it alongside the rest of the loan file.

Start with what the report says and whether the sales behind it support the conclusion.

Who hires the appraiser, and who pays for it?

Your lender orders the appraisal and discloses its fee as a loan cost. On the Loan Estimate the appraisal fee appears in Section B, Services You Cannot Shop For, because the lender picks the provider. Compare that whole section between lenders rather than trying to shop the line item, as the CFPB Loan Estimate explainer describes.

For consumer credit secured by a principal dwelling, Regulation Z prohibits a covered person from causing its assigned value to be based on any factor other than the independent judgment of a person that prepares valuations, and it names seeking a minimum or maximum value as an example. Your agent cannot lean on the appraiser, and neither can your lender. See 12 CFR 1026.42, checked September 12, 2026.

You are entitled to read the result. Under Regulation B a creditor must give an applicant a copy of all appraisals and other written valuations developed in connection with an application for credit that is to be secured by a first lien on a dwelling, promptly upon completion, or three business days prior to consummation of the transaction, whichever is earlier. You can waive the timing, so ask for it early instead.

The same section requires written notice of that right not later than the third business day after the creditor receives an application. A creditor shall not charge an applicant for providing a copy of appraisals and other written valuations, although it may still charge you for the appraisal itself. The rule text sits at 12 CFR 1002.14.

Which comparable sales is the appraiser allowed to use?

For an appraisal supporting a Fannie Mae loan, the Selling Guide sets out the comparable-sales requirements. A minimum of three closed comparables must be reported in the sales comparison approach. Comparable sales that have closed within the last 12 months should be used, though the guide lets an appraiser reach further back when an older sale is the better indicator and the report explains why.

Flood-zone differences also deserve attention on the Gulf Coast. External factors, including Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) designated flood zone, should be given consideration when selecting comparables, per Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-1.3-08, checked September 12, 2026.

If your Gulf Breeze or Perdido Key home sits in a different FEMA flood zone than the sales used to value it, ask how the report considered that difference. A different zone does not automatically disqualify a sale. The useful question is whether the appraiser explained the comparison and any adjustment.

What does a low appraisal cost you in cash?

This is a hypothetical illustration, not a quote. Assume a $415,000 contract, a planned 10 percent down payment, and an appraisal that returns $400,000. It assumes the lender sizes the loan against the lower of the two figures, which is exactly what the low number puts to the test. Closing costs and deposits are left out so the gap arithmetic stays visible.

  • The loan you planned on: 90 percent of $415,000 is $373,500, leaving a $41,500 down payment.
  • The loan under this assumption: 90 percent of $400,000 is $360,000.
  • Cash if the price holds: $415,000 less the loan leaves $55,000 to bring.
  • The additional cash under these assumptions: $13,500 of additional cash, which is the $15,000 value gap multiplied by your loan-to-value ratio.

In this example, the $15,000 value gap increases the buyer's planned cash by $13,500 because the loan stays at 90 percent of the valuation basis. A different loan structure changes the result. Have the lender recalculate the actual loan amount, mortgage insurance, closing costs and cash to close before negotiating.

How do you request a reconsideration of value?

In writing, with evidence, through your lender's own process. Five federal agencies issued interagency guidance on reconsiderations of value on July 18, 2024, defining it as a request to reassess the value of residential real estate. Ask your lender for its procedure and submission deadline. The CFPB announcement sets out its scope.

The CFPB describes what a borrower can raise: factual or other errors or omissions, inadequate comparable properties, or evidence that the appraisal was influenced by prohibited bias. Its consumer guidance is the plainest statement of those grounds.

  1. Get the report itself, not your loan officer's summary of it, and read the comparable grid.
  2. Check each comparable for the things the guide names: distance, flood zone, sale date, room count, site size and condition.
  3. Look for factual errors first. Identify any incorrect square footage, missed bathroom or garage counted as living area, and supply records supporting the correction.
  4. Assemble two or three better closed sales with addresses, close dates and why each is a fairer match than what the report used.
  5. Send it to the lender in one written package and ask for the reconsideration in writing. Ask how the review affects your rate lock and contractual deadlines. A request does not automatically extend either.

A documented error or more appropriate comparable gives the lender something specific to evaluate, but it does not guarantee a higher value. Ask about turnaround time. If the deadline is tight, discuss any needed extension with the lender and the other party before the existing deadline passes.

When will the lender skip the appraisal entirely?

Only when the applicable loan program and the lender permit it. Fannie Mae's automated underwriting can return value acceptance, in which case an appraisal is not required for that loan.

The exclusions are where this gets local. Because Fannie Mae lists leasehold properties as ineligible for a value acceptance offer, a Pensacola Beach or Navarre Beach buyer using that program for leased island land should ask the lender which appraisal is required. Our Pensacola Beach leasehold guide covers what else that lease changes.

Two other limits matter on the water. Value acceptance is not offered where the purchase price or estimated value provided to DU is $1,000,000 or more, and a lender may only use one if the value acceptance offer is not more than four months old on the date of the note, per Selling Guide B4-1.4-10. Two- to four-unit properties are excluded as well.

For a Fannie Mae loan, an Orange Beach or Gulf Shores contract at or above $1,000,000 sits outside value acceptance. Check an Alabama property's appraiser through the Alabama Real Estate Appraisers Board rather than Florida's. In Florida, a certified residential appraiser is a person certified by the department as qualified to issue appraisal reports for residential real property of one to four residential units, under the Real Estate Appraisal Board.

What else should a coastal condo buyer review?

Review the association budget, reserves, inspections and assessments alongside the appraisal. Florida generally requires a structural integrity reserve study at least every 10 years for covered condominium buildings three habitable stories or higher in height. Applicability, exceptions and timing provisions appear in section 718.112(2)(g), Florida Statutes.

A reserve study can identify funding needs that affect an association's budget or assessments. Ask for the latest study and adopted funding plan, and have the lender review project eligibility separately from the unit's appraised value. Confirm the building's applicable deadlines with the association and qualified advisers. Our condo due diligence checklist lists the documents to request first.

What should you do first?

Request the report and the lender's revised numbers first. Work through these three steps before negotiating.

  1. Request the appraisal report from your lender in writing, and read the comparable grid rather than the summary.
  2. Recalculate the cash on the actual numbers so you can compare the added cash with your remaining reserves.
  3. Decide which of the four responses you want before the seller proposes one for you.
